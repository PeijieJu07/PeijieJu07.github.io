Skill-Based Labour Market Segmentation and Mismatch Dynamics

Job Market Paper · Working paper

Whether unemployed workers can fill available vacancies depends partly on how closely their skills match the requirements of those jobs. Yet conventional mismatch measures treat administrative occupation categories as separate search markets. I examine how replacing these boundaries with skill-based groups changes measured mismatch in the UK during 2016–2022. I combine online job advertisements collected by Adzuna, worker employment histories from Understanding Society, and ESCO occupational skill requirements. Nine skill-based occupation clusters reveal larger movements in productivity-weighted mismatch around the COVID-19 pandemic than the nine administrative SOC occupation groups. Over the full sample, the skill-cluster series also has an upward descriptive trend, with increases visible before the pandemic, while the SOC major-group series trends downwards. Detailed occupational estimates also rise, showing that both aggregation and group composition shape the account of reallocation. Holding vacancy shares fixed changes the detailed occupational path more than holding jobseeker shares fixed. These findings are consistent with skill-related allocation difficulties extending beyond the immediate pandemic disruption. They make market definition relevant to the UK productivity debate and motivate attention to continuing retraining and worker reallocation.

Occupational Structure and Occupational Mobility over the Last 30 Years in the UK

Working paper

Using the British Household Panel Survey and the UK Household Longitudinal Study, this paper documents changes in occupational structure and worker mobility between 1991 and 2020. It examines employment polarisation, movements between occupations, and differences across occupations in wages and automation exposure. Approximately half of observed job transitions occur across occupations.

Mental Health and Job Mobility

Work in progress

This paper examines the relationship between psychological distress and job mobility using the UK Household Longitudinal Study. Pooled and individual fixed-effects estimates indicate positive associations between GHQ-12 scores and job changes. Weak-instrument diagnostics and unresolved exclusion restrictions mean that the current evidence does not support a precise causal estimate.
